# EMANE experiment results

Generated by `emane/analysis/analyze.py` from the raw files in `emane/results/*/results/`.

Setup: 16 NEMs, slot 1000 us (overhead 100 us), 2.4 GHz, 12 Mbit/s (1350 B/slot), 10 MHz, noise floor -100.0 dBm. Unit-disk channel (E1-E4): 30 dBm, pathloss 90 dB on 500 m links and 200 dB otherwise. E5: free-space pathloss for every pair, 3 dBm. Traffic: Poisson UDP broadcast, 64 B payload, 20 s; IPv6 disabled in the nodes; traffic starts only after every NEM reports the full schedule accepted.

*Frames received in the sender's slot*: for every directed link (t -> r) whose slot at r is used by t alone, EMANE's RxSlotStatusTable at r (frames received in t's slot) divided by EMANE's PHY transmit count at t. It compares the Brain's plan with physical events at both ends and does not use EMANE's installed-schedule tables. *Stray receptions*: frames counted at a receiver in slots where none of its neighbours is scheduled (valid must be 0; missed = reception overlapping a slot boundary because of host timing jitter).

EMANE logs 'Unable to set Real Time Priority' in every run: realtime scheduling needs CAP_SYS_NICE in the initial user namespace, which a rootless run inside `unshare --user` cannot have. EMANE's own documentation names missed slots as the symptom of insufficient timing fidelity; the residual losses in E1/E1b are of this kind (no SINR drops, no RX-during-TX errors). TxSlotStatusTable counts TX *opportunities* of the installed schedule, so 'installed TX slots = plan' only confirms schedule installation, not transmission behaviour.
